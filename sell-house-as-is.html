The current condition matters

As-is does not mean hiding property problems.

An as-is review begins with an honest description of the property. Known repairs, visible damage, incomplete work, access concerns, and occupancy details should be shared as clearly as possible.

Selling as-is describes the property's present condition. It does not remove disclosure, ownership, title, documentation, or written transaction requirements.

Describe the Property

Instead of improving the house for public marketing, you can explain what it needs and allow those conditions to be considered during an individual direct-sale review.

Traditional preparation may involve contractors, cleaning crews, landscaping, staging, photography, repeated access, and uncertainty about which improvements will influence a buyer. An as-is route may reduce that preparation when a direct purchase option is available.

The property's condition still influences any potential terms. Structural concerns, major systems, water damage, unfinished renovations, deferred maintenance, exterior deterioration, and remaining contents can all affect the review.

Repair needs

Explain known damage, outdated systems, unfinished work, and maintenance that has been postponed.

Cleaning and belongings

Describe remaining furniture, stored belongings, debris, or cleaning concerns that may affect access or possession.

Access and occupancy

Share who currently occupies the house and how property access can reasonably be coordinated.

The property can be described as it exists today, including unfinished work and visible maintenance concerns.

Preparation you may avoid

Do not renovate only to begin the conversation.

A direct as-is review may help homeowners who do not want to coordinate improvement projects, prepare every room for photography, or manage a lengthy pre-listing schedule.

  • Cosmetic updates can be reported instead of completed for public presentation.
  • Known repair work can be described before determining whether a direct option is available.
  • The house does not need to be styled for listing photographs or public open houses.
  • Exterior cleanup, overgrowth, fencing, and landscaping can be included in the condition discussion.

Interior condition

Describe flooring, walls, ceilings, kitchens, bathrooms, fixtures, odors, damage, and rooms that are unfinished or difficult to access.
