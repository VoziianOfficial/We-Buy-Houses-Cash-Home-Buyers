A direct-sale review replaces public marketing preparation with a focused exchange of property information.

The first stage is not a valuation promise or automatic offer. It is an opportunity to explain the property and the circumstances affecting a possible sale. The team may request clarification when important details are missing.

When a direct option is available, written terms can be considered before the seller decides whether to continue. Ownership, title, documentation, access, possession, and closing requirements remain part of the transaction.

A different selling route

Fewer public steps does not mean fewer important details.

A direct sale may avoid listing photography, public showings, staging, and traditional buyer financing. The transaction still requires accurate information, accepted written terms, title work, and closing coordination.

  • The property does not need a public marketing launch before the initial review.
  • Known repairs can be explained instead of completed before submitting the request.
  • Potential terms are considered only after the property information has been reviewed.
  • Closing is coordinated after written acceptance and required title or transaction work.

Review checkpoints

What the team may need to understand.

Complete information can make the initial review clearer, while missing ownership, access, occupancy, or title details may require additional communication.

Location

Exact property address

The street address allows the team to understand the neighborhood, property type, and local market context.

Condition

Known repairs and deferred maintenance

Structural concerns, major systems, damage, unfinished work, and general maintenance can affect the review.

Access

Property access and current occupancy

Owners, tenants, relatives, vacancy, security, and scheduling restrictions may influence the process.

Ownership

Ownership and title circumstances

Multiple owners, inherited property, liens, judgments, and unresolved records may require additional review.

Timing

Preferred selling and possession timeline

Explain when you would prefer to close and when the property could reasonably be transferred.

Different property situations

The process adapts to the information you provide.

Some properties require additional clarification because of occupancy, title, access, condition, or ownership circumstances.

Inherited property

Ownership documents, probate status, multiple heirs, and property access may affect the review and closing path.

Tenant-occupied house

The current lease, access arrangements, possession expectations, and tenant communication may need to be understood.

Property needing repairs

Known damage, unfinished work, outdated systems, and deferred maintenance can be described before the review.

Vacant property

Security, access, remaining belongings, utilities, and deferred exterior care may require clarification.

Relocation

A moving schedule or employment change can be included when explaining the preferred transaction timeline.
